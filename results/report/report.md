# CLARA OLR from ERA5 and coordinates: spatio-temporal GP kernels compared

Generated by `src/report.py` on 2026-10-26 13:10 from the outputs listed in the status table at the end. Grid 5° × month; target: monthly-mean CLARA night-side OLR per cell.

## 1. How do CLARA and ERA5 differ?
Structural and physical comparison: [../eda/inventory/dataset_comparison.md](../eda/inventory/dataset_comparison.md). In short:
- **Structure.** CLARA is an instantaneous 30 s radiometer series along one satellite's track, located by its boresight footprint. ERA5 is a complete 0.25° reanalysis grid of monthly means.
- **Physics.** CLARA measures night-side OLR. It is not angle-corrected and is contaminated in daylight. ERA5 `ttr` is a 24 h accumulation, negative upward; we use its night-time monthly mean in W m⁻².
- **Statistics** (EDA, sampling-matched cell-months):
  - **Pooled, CLARA vs ERA5 night:** r = 0.333, bias -62.9 W m⁻², RMSE 77.4 W m⁻², slope 0.42.
  - **By period:** pre-gap bias -77.1 W m⁻², r 0.44; post-gap bias -35.6 W m⁻², r 0.41. The step sits inside the 2021-09 → 2022-05 gap (temporal_global.png). Within each period CLARA does follow the ERA5 sampling-matched monthly fluctuations.
  - **Reference check, ERA5 24 h vs CERES on the same cell-months:** bias +2.4 W m⁻², r 0.993, RMSE 4.7 W m⁻². ERA5 and the independent observations agree, so the CLARA–ERA5 discrepancies belong to CLARA (calibration, geometry, sampling).
  - **Spatial structure is heavily damped in CLARA.** Its zonal-mean profile has the right shape (r = 0.93 with ERA5 night) but only 56% of ERA5's equator-to-pole range (spatial_zonal.png). Its mean map shows little of ERA5's regional structure (spatial_means.png).
- Figures: [../eda/physics_sza.png](../eda/physics_sza.png), [../eda/physics_vza.png](../eda/physics_vza.png), [../eda/temporal_global.png](../eda/temporal_global.png), [../eda/spatial_zonal.png](../eda/spatial_zonal.png).

## 2. What preprocessing made them comparable?
All decisions with reasons: [../preprocessing/decisions.md](../preprocessing/decisions.md).
- **Units and time.** ERA5 converted to W m⁻² positive upward from the hour-of-day product, validated against the monthly product (≤ 0.01 W m⁻²). Both datasets aligned on calendar months.
- **QC.** Night-side footprints (SZA > 100°) and VZA ≤ 60° only.
- **Aggregation.** CLARA binned into 5° cell-months with overpass counts; never interpolated.
- **Prediction grid.** Every lat-lon cell of every month. Polar-day cells (no night) use the darkest-hours ERA5 fallback, are flagged `night_fallback` and are extrapolation (CLARA never observes them).
- **Pairing.** ERA5 reduced to night-time hours, area-averaged to the same cells, optionally cap-smoothed to CLARA's effective footprint (radius chosen per CV fold).
- **Missingness.** Kept explicit: an observation table plus a prediction grid with a `clara_observed` flag.
- **Standardisation.** Only inside each CV fold, with training rows.

## 3. Spatial and temporal structure in CLARA OLR
- **Along track:** CLARA samples correlate at r = 0.88 at 232 km, and the CLARA − ERA5 residual reaches 1/e at ~1,933 km. Neighbouring samples share most of their signal: a wide footprint plus synoptic weather.
- **Spatial residual correlogram** (CLARA minus a linear ERA5 fit, month means removed): fitted 0.35·exp(−d / 1,056 km). The structured part has a range of ~1,056 km, and the nugget (sampling noise) takes ~65% of the residual variance (spatial_correlogram.png). Pre-gap range 1,201 km, post-gap 943 km.
- **Temporal residual autocorrelation** at a fixed cell (same residual as the spatial correlogram; the persistent spatial pattern is included): lag 1 month r = 0.15, lag 2 months r = 0.09, lag 6 months r = 0.01, lag 12 months r = 0.11. Relative to the noise-free part (≈ 0.35 at zero distance), this measures how long the structured discrepancy at a location persists.
- **Day to day** (daily cell means of CLARA − hour-matched ERA5, period means removed): lag-1 day r = 0.30; the within-cell-month SD of daily means is 34 W m⁻². This is weather and noise that monthly ERA5 cannot explain; the noise term absorbs it.

## Statistical model
Every kernel and every CV fold uses the same model; only the correlation function κ changes. Index $i$ is a cell-month: one 5° × 5° cell in one calendar month.

### Regression (mean function)

$$
\mathrm{OLR}^{\mathrm{CLARA}}_i = \underbrace{\beta_0 + \beta_1\,\mathrm{OLR}^{\mathrm{ERA5}}_i + \beta_2\,s_i}_{\text{regression}} + \underbrace{f(\mathbf{x}_i)}_{\text{GP}} + \varepsilon_i
$$

- $y_i \equiv \mathrm{OLR}^{\mathrm{CLARA}}_i$ is the mean of the quality-controlled CLARA samples in the cell-month (W m⁻²). They come from $n_i$ distinct overpasses.
- $e_i \equiv \mathrm{OLR}^{\mathrm{ERA5}}_i$ is the night-time ERA5 OLR of the same cell and month, smoothed over spherical caps of radius $R$. $R \in \lbrace 0, 300, 600, 1000, 1500 \rbrace$ km is chosen in each fold as the radius with the highest correlation with CLARA on the training rows.
- $s_i = \mathbf{1}[t_i \ge \text{2021-09}]$ is the post-gap indicator. $\beta_2$ is the calibration step across the data gap at fixed ERA5, $\beta_0$ the pre-gap intercept and $\beta_1$ the change in CLARA per W m⁻² of ERA5.
- In vector form the mean is $\mathbf{h}_i^\top\boldsymbol\beta$, with $\mathbf{h}_i = (1, e_i, s_i)^\top$ and $\boldsymbol\beta = (\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2)^\top$.

### Gaussian process on the residual
The regression residual is split into a spatio-temporally correlated part $f$ and independent noise $\varepsilon$, with $f$ independent of $\varepsilon$:

$$
y_i - \mathbf{h}_i^\top\boldsymbol\beta = f(\mathbf{x}_i) + \varepsilon_i, \qquad f \sim \mathcal{GP}\big(0,\ k_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')\big), \qquad \varepsilon_i \overset{\text{ind}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\ \sigma_0^2 + \frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_i}\right)
$$

- **Noise.** $\sigma_1^2$ is the sampling variance of one overpass. Samples along one overpass are strongly correlated, so the overpass is the independent unit and this part shrinks as $1/n_i$. $\sigma_0^2$ is noise that does not average out with more overpasses.
- **Joint likelihood.** Stacked over the $N$ training cell-months:

$$
\mathbf{y} \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{H}\boldsymbol\beta,\ \mathbf{K}_\theta + \mathbf{D}\big), \qquad [\mathbf{K}_\theta]_{ij} = k_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j), \qquad \mathbf{D} = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2/n_i\right)
$$

  $\boldsymbol\beta$ and $\theta$ are estimated **jointly** from this likelihood. It is not a two-step fit (regression first, then a GP on its residuals): as in generalised least squares, the regression coefficients account for the correlation of the residuals.

### Covariance function
Each cell-month enters through $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{c}, t)$:

$$
\mathbf{u} = (\cos\varphi\cos\lambda,\ \cos\varphi\sin\lambda,\ \sin\varphi), \qquad \mathbf{c} = (\cos 2\pi\tau,\ \sin 2\pi\tau), \qquad t = \text{years since 2020-01-01}
$$

Here $\varphi, \lambda$ are the latitude and longitude of the cell centre, and $\tau \in [0, 1)$ is the fraction of the year at mid-month. $\lVert\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}'\rVert = 2\sin(d / 2R_\oplus)$ is the chord between two cells $d$ km apart on the sphere (Earth radius $R_\oplus$ = 6371 km), so the poles and the dateline need no special case. On the seasonal circle $\mathbf{c}$, December is next to January. The covariance has two additive components:

$$
k_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') = \sigma_A^2\,\kappa(r_A) + \sigma_B^2\,\kappa(r_B)
$$

$$
r_A^2 = \frac{\lVert\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}'\rVert^2}{\ell_{\text{space},A}^2} + \frac{\lVert\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{c}'\rVert^2}{\ell_{\text{season}}^2}, \qquad r_B^2 = \frac{\lVert\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{u}'\rVert^2}{\ell_{\text{space},B}^2} + \frac{(t - t')^2}{\ell_{\text{time}}^2}
$$

- **Component A (space × season)** is a CLARA − ERA5 discrepancy pattern that recurs every year.
- **Component B (space × long-term time)** holds changes that do not recur: drift beyond the step, and any regional structure of the step.
- $\theta = (\sigma_A, \ell_{\text{space},A}, \ell_{\text{season}}, \sigma_B, \ell_{\text{space},B}, \ell_{\text{time}}, \sigma_0, \sigma_1)$.

The three kernels differ only in κ:

| kernel | κ(r) | sample paths of f |
|---|---|---|
| Exponential | $e^{-r}$ | continuous, not differentiable |
| Gaussian (SE) | $e^{-r^2/2}$ | infinitely differentiable |
| Matérn 3/2 | $(1 + \sqrt{3}\,r)\,e^{-\sqrt{3}\,r}$ | once differentiable |

All three are valid covariances here because $r_A$ and $r_B$ are scaled Euclidean distances (chords). With great-circle distance, the Gaussian and Matérn 3/2 kernels would not be positive definite on the sphere.

### Prediction
At a cell-month $\mathbf{x}_*$ with ERA5 $e_*$ and step $s_*$, the target is the monthly-mean CLARA OLR $\mu_* = \mathbf{h}_*^\top\boldsymbol\beta + f(\mathbf{x}_*)$. For an exact GP its posterior is

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mu_* \mid \mathbf{y}] = \mathbf{h}_*^\top\hat{\boldsymbol\beta} + \mathbf{k}_*^\top(\mathbf{K}_\theta + \mathbf{D})^{-1}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{H}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}), \qquad \operatorname{Var}[\mu_* \mid \mathbf{y}] = k_\theta(\mathbf{x}_*, \mathbf{x}_*) - \mathbf{k}_*^\top(\mathbf{K}_\theta + \mathbf{D})^{-1}\mathbf{k}_*
$$

with $[\mathbf{k}_*]_i = k_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_*)$. The fitted model uses the sparse version of the same formulas (next subsection):

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mu_* \mid \mathbf{y}] = \mathbf{h}_*^\top\hat{\boldsymbol\beta} + \mathbf{k}_{*u}\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{K}_{uf}\mathbf{D}^{-1}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{H}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}), \qquad \operatorname{Var}[\mu_* \mid \mathbf{y}] = k_{**} - \mathbf{k}_{*u}\mathbf{K}_{uu}^{-1}\mathbf{k}_{u*} + \mathbf{k}_{*u}\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{k}_{u*}, \qquad \boldsymbol\Sigma = \big(\mathbf{K}_{uu} + \mathbf{K}_{uf}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{K}_{fu}\big)^{-1}
$$

- **Maps.** The uncertainty maps show the latent SD, $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[\mu_* \mid \mathbf{y}]}$.
- **Scoring.** Against a held-out CLARA cell-month from $n_*$ overpasses (interval coverage, CRPS, NLPD), the noise is added: $\operatorname{Var}[y_* \mid \mathbf{y}] = \operatorname{Var}[\mu_* \mid \mathbf{y}] + \sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2 / n_*$.
- **Plug-in.** $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ and $\hat\theta$ are plugged in; their uncertainty is not propagated.

### Estimation: sparse variational GP
An exact GP costs $O(N^3)$ time and $O(N^2)$ memory, and $N$ reaches 37,898 training cell-months in the full fit. The fit therefore uses the variational approximation of Titsias (2009) with $M = 1000$ inducing inputs $\mathbf{Z}$. These are k-means centres of the training inputs, fixed during the fit and shared by the three kernels within a fold. $\boldsymbol\beta$ and $\theta$ maximise a lower bound on the log marginal likelihood, extended here to the heteroscedastic noise $\mathbf{D}$:

$$
\log p(\mathbf{y} \mid \boldsymbol\beta, \theta) \;\ge\; \mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol\beta, \theta) = \log \mathcal{N}\big(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{H}\boldsymbol\beta,\ \mathbf{Q}_{ff} + \mathbf{D}\big) - \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\big(\mathbf{D}^{-1}(\mathbf{K}_{ff} - \mathbf{Q}_{ff})\big), \qquad \mathbf{Q}_{ff} = \mathbf{K}_{fu}\mathbf{K}_{uu}^{-1}\mathbf{K}_{uf}
$$

- **Optimiser.** L-BFGS (type-II maximum likelihood), started from fixed, data-independent values. The starts scale the initial length scales by ×1, ×0.5, ×2, and the best bound wins.
- **Exactness check.** With $\mathbf{Z}$ equal to the training inputs, $\mathcal{F}$ equals the exact log marginal likelihood (unit test).
- **Standardisation.** $y$ and $e$ are standardised with training statistics inside each fit. All reported values are in physical units.

Same noise model, same step and same folds:

| constant per period (no GP, no ERA5) | $\beta_0 + \beta_2 s$ | $\mathbf{D}$ |

Without the GP ($f \equiv 0$) the likelihood is exact. For fixed $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1)$, $\boldsymbol\beta$ is the GLS estimate $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta} = (\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{H})^{-1}\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{y}$, and $(\sigma_0, \sigma_1)$ maximise the profile likelihood. The latent variance of a prediction is $\mathbf{h}_*^\top(\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{H})^{-1}\mathbf{h}_*$.

Everything learned from data is learned per CV fold, on its training rows only: $R$, the standardisation, $\mathbf{Z}$, $\boldsymbol\beta$ and $\theta$.

How to read them:
- **β₁** is how many W m⁻² CLARA changes per W m⁻² of night-time ERA5. Values well below 1 match the damped spatial contrasts seen in the EDA.
- **β₂** is the calibration step: the change in CLARA's level from the start of the 2021-09 → 2022-05 gap on, at fixed ERA5. β₀ is the pre-gap intercept and β₀ + β₂ the post-gap one.
- **Component A** (space × season) is the recurring, seasonal CLARA–ERA5 discrepancy. ℓ_space,A is its horizontal scale; ℓ_season is how quickly the pattern changes through the year.
- **Component B** (space × long-term time) absorbs drift beyond the step, and any regional structure of the step. A short ℓ_time and a large σ_B mean the model needs non-recurring temporal flexibility.
- **σ₀ and σ₁** are the noise: σ₀ is irreducible discrepancy (residual geometry effects, weather the monthly ERA5 cannot see); σ₁/√n is the sampling error of a cell-month mean from n overpasses.
- Per-fold estimates (spread across blocks): `results/model/<kernel>/hyperparameters_<cv_type>.csv`.

## 9. Limitations
- **Calibration step.** The ~40 W m⁻² calibration change inside the 2021-09 → 2022-05 gap enters the mean function as one global offset β₂ from 2021-09 on. When in the gap it happened is unknown: by convention the gap months take the post-gap level, so the maps of those months (never observed) are extrapolation, and their uncertainty does not include the timing of the step. A step that differs between regions is left to the long-term GP component.
- **Geometry.** CLARA is not angle-corrected. Within VZA ≤ 60° the residual viewing-angle effect is tens of W m⁻² and is treated as noise because VZA is not an allowed predictor. The drift of altitude and local time is also absorbed by the time components.
- **Smoothing and scale.** CLARA's footprint is wide (field of view not documented). ERA5 is matched by spherical-cap smoothing (~10³ km optimum), but CLARA's damped spatial contrasts mean the map's fine-scale structure is mostly ERA5's, scaled by β₁. It is not information CLARA resolves.
- **Missing observations.** Cell-months are observed only along night-side tracks; polar-day cells have no night and are predicted from the darkest-hours ERA5 fallback (extrapolation, hatched). Post-gap cell-months typically rest on a single overpass, so they are much noisier than pre-gap ones.
- **Temporal meaning.** Monthly means of sparse instantaneous samples are compared with complete monthly means: day-to-day weather (~34 W m⁻² within cell-month SD of daily means) is absorbed as noise.
- **Approximation.** The sparse (VFE) GP uses M = 1000 fixed k-means inducing inputs, shared by all kernels in a fold. The approximation was not compared with the exact GP; in the CV folds there is about one per 35, and the effect of M at that scale was not measured. Kernel differences of a few tenths of a W m⁻² may therefore partly reflect the approximation.
- **Mean-coefficient uncertainty.** β₀ and β₁ are point estimates (type-II maximum likelihood); their uncertainty is not propagated. With ~3×10⁴ training cell-months it is small relative to the other terms.
